A warm, bittersweet harbor scene at golden hour. An enormous ancient warship — a legendary Greco-Roman galley with a high carved prow, patched hull planking and repaired scars all over its flanks — sits permanently moored against a stone quay, clearly never to sail again. Its rigging has been strung with lanterns and drying laundry; a broad gangplank has been widened into a staircase; crates and barrels of beer are being rolled down into the hold through an open cargo hatch. A hand-painted wooden sign hangs from the rail. Glass cases of relics, trophies, broken weapons and a titan's tooth are displayed along the open deck like a museum.

Standing at the top of the gangplank with his arms spread wide in an innkeeper's welcome, a foaming tankard in one fist, visibly proud: powerful male minotaur with a muscular build, broad shoulders, and brown fur. His bovine head features large, curved dark grey horns, piercing glowing blue eyes, and a bull snout. He has stylish, thick blonde hair swept back and a well-groomed blonde fur beard. He is dressed in a draped white toga with an ornate red sash featuring carved patterns and a textured grey shoulder strap. The aesthetic is a Greco-Roman gladiator and mythological creature.

Around him, ordinary townsfolk of a rebuilt Greek-fantasy port city climb aboard to drink — dockworkers, soldiers out of armor, children peering into the display cases. On the main mast, faint and translucent, the departing figure of a dryad woman made of leaves, bark-textured skin and green light is lifting away from the wood and dissolving into the evening air, one hand raised in farewell.

Behind the ship, a city under reconstruction: scaffolding on marble temples, cranes, fresh white stone against fire-blackened walls.
