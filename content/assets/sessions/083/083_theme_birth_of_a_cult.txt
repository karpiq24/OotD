A quiet, warm scene in the corner of a rubble-strewn Greco-Roman marketplace weeks after a great battle. In a side alcove between two cracked marble columns stands a small, crude homemade shrine: a shoddy box knocked together from mismatched planks, sheltering a lumpy figurine hammered out of a scrap of metal, clearly meant to represent a horned bull-headed warrior holding an axe. The craftsmanship is poor and utterly sincere.

At the foot of the shrine, offerings have piled up: a full beer tankard, scattered copper coins, a cup of wine, locks of braided hair, and small rolled parchments tied with string — written prayers asking for a husband's return from war, for a child, for a curse to be lifted.

An old woman in a simple homespun chiton kneels before it, hands together, eyes closed. A child watches from behind a column, holding a second tankard.

Background: rebuilt market stalls, swept rubble, morning light slanting between the columns, other half-finished shrines visible further down the street.

Warm golden light, intimate scale, bittersweet and reverent mood, a sense of a religion being born out of gratitude. High quality digital fantasy art, highly detailed.
